Learning Guide
From zero to C1

Language Levels

English levels according to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), recognized worldwide in schools, universities and exams such as IELTS. and next to each level is the approximate number of hours to move up from the previous level.

  1. A1
    Beginner90–100 hours From zero to A1

    You understand and use very simple everyday phrases, introduce yourself, and ask and answer about simple things like your name and where you live.

  2. A2
    Elementary90–100 hours from A1 to A2

    You talk about familiar topics like family, shopping and work, and handle simple everyday situations.

  3. B1
    Pre-intermediate170–200 hours from A2 to B1

    You understand the main ideas on familiar topics, manage when travelling, and express your opinion and plans briefly.

  4. B2
    Upper-intermediate150–200 hours from B1 to B2

    You understand more complex texts and conversations, and speak fluently and spontaneously with native speakers without much difficulty.

  5. C1
    Advancedabout 200 hours from B2 to C1

    You understand long, difficult texts, and express yourself fluently and flexibly in study, work and social life.

What’s the total?

From zero to C1 you need about 700–800 hours. The numbers are estimates and vary from person to person depending on consistency.

Source: Cambridge English — Guided learning hours

How to use your time for each skill?

  • 📚
    Reading

    The most important skill, so give it the most time. Read stories slightly above your level, and tap a word you don’t know to see its meaning, example and audio.

  • 📇
    Memorizing words

    Learn new words every day with the cards, and review old ones with quizzes so you don’t forget them.

  • 🎧
    Listening

    Listen to podcasts or videos slightly above your level, preferably with no subtitles, just listening, or at least English subtitles.

  • 🗣️
    Speaking

    Speak English every day, even for a few minutes, to yourself or to someone, and don’t be afraid of mistakes.

The secret? Consistency.

One hour every day is better than seven hours once a week.
